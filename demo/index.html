How to Play the Snake Arcade Game

  1. Move — use the arrow keys or W A S D on desktop; swipe on mobile.
  2. Eat — every apple makes the snake longer and the score higher.
  3. Survive — hitting the wall or your own tail ends the round.
  4. Pick your speed — Slug (slow), Worm (normal) or Python (fast).

Classic Snake Game

Snake has been rebuilt a thousand times, and most of those rebuilds changed the rules. This version keeps the original ones.

  • Walls end the round. There is no wrap-around and no exit at the edges. Touch the border and the run is over.
  • The grid stays visible. One move equals one square, so you can always count your way out of trouble. That is what makes planning possible instead of just reacting.
  • Every apple is worth one point. No power-ups, no multipliers, no combos. Your score is simply how many apples you ate before you made a mistake.
  • Three speeds, picked before you start. Slug (150 ms per step), Worm (100 ms) and Python (50 ms). Slower speeds are for learning the board; faster ones are where the game gets interesting.
  • Your best score stays on your device. It is saved locally in your browser, so it survives a refresh and never leaves your machine.

That is the whole game — nothing to unlock, nothing to buy. The only thing that changes from run to run is how long you last.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the old snake game called?

The game is simply called Snake, and it was also widely known as Nibbles in its MS-DOS era. The genre traces back to the 1976 arcade title Blockade, released by Gremlin Industries, where the goal was to survive longer than the other player.

Can you still play snake?

Yes — and you do not need to install anything. The snake arcade game on this page runs directly in your browser on desktop and mobile. You can also find a version hidden inside Google search.

What is that snake game called?

Depending on where you first played it, you may know it as Snake, Nokia Snake, or Nibbles. They are all the same core idea: steer a growing line, eat the food, and avoid hitting the walls or your own tail.

Was snake an arcade game?

It belongs to the arcade tradition. The block-style snake mechanic began with the 1976 arcade game Blockade, and the format became a staple of arcade cabinets, early home computers and mobile phones before moving to the browser.
